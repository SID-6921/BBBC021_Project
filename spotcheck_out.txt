BUDGET: All deep-learning models were optimized with Adam and cross-entropy loss (softmax over the 10 classes; batch size 16). The compact CNN trained for 6 epochs at a learning rate of 1×10⁻³. The pretrained ResNet-18 used staged fine-tuning: in stage 1, all layers except the final fully connected layer were frozen and trained for 10 epochs at 1×10⁻³; in stage 2, the last two residual blocks were unfrozen and fine-tuned for 6 further epochs at 1×10⁻⁴, for a total training budget of 16 epochs. In our original submission, the randomly initialized ResNet-18 was trained for only 10 epochs at 1×10⁻³ with no second stage -- a training-budget asymmetry that confounds the scratch-vs-pretrained comparison, independent of pretraining itself. In this revision, the scratch ResNet-18 is trained for the same total budget of 16 epochs at 1×10⁻³ (all layers trainable throughout, since there is no pretrained representation to protect behind a frozen stage), so that the comparison isolates the effect of pretraining rather than the effect of additional gradient updates. No class weighting, resampling, or data augmentation was applied to any deep-learning model; the checkpoint used for test-set evaluation is the model state at the end of training (no early stopping), for all models.

BUDGET: Once compound-level leakage and channel fusion are both removed, the pretrained ResNet-18 remains the strongest model (mean accuracy 0.721, mean AUC 0.965 across five seeds), and pretraining strategy remains a larger determinant of performance than the choice between feature-based and deep-learning approaches in this small-sample regime. However, the magnitude of every headline number changes substantially from our original submission: the pretrained model's accuracy falls from 0.939 to 0.721, and the scratch-vs-pretrained accuracy gap, which we originally attributed entirely to pretraining, is now attributable to a combination of pretraining, a training-budget asymmetry we did not intend (Section 2.3), and, most importantly, a compound-level leakage effect that was likely the single largest contributor to the originally reported gap between pixel-based deep models and feature-based baselines.
